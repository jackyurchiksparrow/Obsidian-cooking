### Sourdough starter microbiology

**Who lives there:** two groups of organisms, LAB and yeasts.

- **LAB** come in three types: homofermentative, facultatively heterofermentative, obligately heterofermentative (details below).
- The exact species in any given starter are unknown without lab testing.

Bacteria thrive on sugar.

#### Where the sugar comes from

1. **Flour starch → maltose.** The flour’s own amylase enzymes break damaged starch into maltose once water is added.
2. **Maltose → glucose.** _F. sanfranciscensis_ prefers maltose and splits it, releasing glucose. _K. humilis_ can’t use maltose, so it lives on that released glucose plus the flour’s own small amount of free sugar.
3. **Sucrose → glucose + fructose.** Flour has a little sucrose. Enzymes (from yeast and flour) split it.
4. **Fructose → acetic acid.** Heterofermentative LAB use fructose as a helper molecule. Whenever fructose is available, they make acetic acid instead of ethanol. Fructose is the main lever on acetic acid.

#### Bacteria (LAB)

- **Typical species:** _Fructilactobacillus sanfranciscensis_ (formerly _Lactobacillus sanfranciscensis_). Also common: _L. plantarum_, _L. brevis_.
- **Three types by what they make from sugar:**
    - _Homofermentative_ → almost only lactic acid.
    - _Facultatively heterofermentative_ → lactic acid from glucose, can switch pathway for other sugars.
    - _Obligately heterofermentative_ → lactic acid + acetic acid + ethanol + CO₂. This is the usual type in sourdough, including _F. sanfranciscensis_.

#### Yeasts

- **Typical species:** _Kazachstania humilis_ (formerly _Candida milleri_). Also _Saccharomyces cerevisiae_ and others.
- **Make:** CO₂ + ethanol. Almost no acid.

#### What the products do

|Product|Made by|Effect|
|---|---|---|
|CO₂|Mainly yeast (some from heterofermentative LAB)|Expands the dough|
|Lactic acid|LAB|Lowers pH; mild sourness; no smell|
|Acetic acid|Heterofermentative LAB (more when fructose is available)|Sharp, vinegary; volatile, so you smell it; strongly inhibits yeast|
|Ethanol|Yeast, heterofermentative LAB|Flavour precursors; mostly evaporates in baking|
|Acid → gluten “tightening”|—|? (author’s claim, not verified)|

#### Ranges (Gänzle et al. 1998, one strain each, lab medium, not dough)

| |Bacteria (_F. sanfranciscensis_)|Yeast (_K. humilis_)|
|---|---|---|
|Optimum temp|32–33 °C|27 °C|
|Max temp (no growth)|41 °C|36 °C|
|Min temp (no growth)|~3–4 °C ?|~8 °C ?|
|pH for growth|3.9–6.7, best ~5.5|Unaffected 3.5–7|
|Salt (NaCl)|Stopped at 4%|Tolerates 8%|
|Acetic acid tolerance|High (>160 mmol/L)|Stopped at ~150 mmol/L|

- Below ~26 °C, both respond to temperature the same way, so 20–26 °C doesn’t favour either.
- Above ~27 °C, the yeast slows first, and acid-heavy, yeast-poor starters result.
- **?** = extrapolated from a model, not measured.
