| **--- 1. Cut 10 grams kombu with scissors to weigh it. Don't wash it: the white powder on the surface is flavour, not dirt. For a deeper flavor, soak it overnight in the fridge instead (put the pot or a jar in the fridge). Soaking lets the kombu release flavor before any heat is applied. ---**<br>**--- 3. Watch for small bubbles forming on the bottom and sides of the pot. Take the kombu out at that point, before the water boils. Boiling kombu makes the stock slimy and slightly bitter. | **--- 1.  Put the used kombu and flakes back in the pot with twice less water than was used for the first dashi. Bring to a boil, then lower to a gentle simmer. Simmer gently for 10 minutes. The leftovers have given up their delicate flavor already, so now longer heat is needed to extract the stronger remainder. ---**<br>**--- 2.  In the last minute, you can add 5 grams extra katsuobushi for second dashi (optional) to make the second dashi richer.
